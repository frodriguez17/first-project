Why a HELOC?

Key Benefits

Leverage your home's equity with a flexible credit line designed around your life — not a one-size-fits-all loan.

Flexible Access to Funds

Draw funds as you need them during the draw period — up to your credit limit — and repay only what you borrow. Your line stays open so you can borrow again without reapplying.

Interest Only on What You Use

During the draw period, you typically make interest-only payments on your outstanding balance — keeping monthly costs low while you maintain access to the full line.

Competitive Variable Rates

HELOCs typically carry lower rates than credit cards or personal loans because your home serves as collateral — giving you access to larger sums at a fraction of the cost.

Potential Tax Advantages

Interest paid on a HELOC may be tax-deductible when funds are used to buy, build, or substantially improve your home. Consult a tax advisor for your specific situation.

Fast, Streamlined Process

AmeriSave's digital application lets you get a decision faster. With over 23 years in business and 730K+ clients served, we've made the process as simple as possible.

Reusable Credit Line

As you pay down your balance, your available credit is replenished — just like a credit card but with your home's equity and far lower rates. Borrow, repay, and borrow again.

Smart Uses for a HELOC

From home improvements to emergency reserves — a HELOC is one of the most versatile financial tools available to homeowners.

Home Improvements

Kitchen remodels, bathroom upgrades, additions — invest in your property and potentially increase its value.

Debt Consolidation

Pay off high-interest credit cards or personal loans by consolidating into one lower-rate HELOC payment.

Education Expenses

Fund college tuition or continuing education at rates often lower than private student loans.

Emergency Fund

Establish a safety net without paying interest until you actually need to draw on the line.

Medical Expenses

Cover unexpected healthcare costs or planned procedures without depleting your savings account.

Investment Properties

Use your equity as a down payment on a rental or investment property to grow your portfolio.

Do You Qualify?

Eligibility Requirements

Most homeowners with equity built up in their primary or secondary residence can qualify.

Sufficient Home Equity

Typically, lenders require at least 15–20% equity in your home (combined loan-to-value ratio of 80–85%). AmeriSave can finance up to 90% CLTV.

Credit Score Requirements

A minimum credit score of 620 is generally required, though better rates are available to borrowers with scores of 700 or higher.

Verifiable Income

You'll need to demonstrate stable, verifiable income. A debt-to-income (DTI) ratio below 43% is typically required.

Primary or Secondary Residence

HELOCs are available on owner-occupied primary residences and some secondary residences. Investment properties may have different eligibility criteria.

Satisfactory Payment History

No recent foreclosures, bankruptcies, or serious delinquencies. Consistent mortgage payment history strengthens your application.

Minimum Draw Amount

HELOCs typically have a minimum draw requirement at closing. The maximum line is determined by your available equity and creditworthiness.

Full Picture

HELOC Pros & Cons

Make an informed decision with a clear look at both sides of a HELOC.

Pros

  • Flexibility: Only borrow what you need; your credit line is reusable throughout the draw period.
  • Lower Initial Rates: Variable rates are often lower than credit cards or unsecured personal loans.
  • Interest-Only Payments: Keep cash flow manageable during the draw period by paying only interest.
  • Potential Tax Deduction: Interest may be deductible if funds are used to substantially improve your home (consult a tax professional).
  • No Interest Until You Draw: The credit line costs nothing while idle — useful as an emergency backup.

Cons

  • Variable Rate Risk: Your rate can rise if the Prime Rate increases, making future payments less predictable.
  • Home as Collateral: Failing to repay could put your home at risk of foreclosure.
  • Payment Increase at Repayment: Once the draw period ends, payments jump from interest-only to full P&I, which can be a shock.
  • Possible Freezing of Credit Line: Lenders can reduce or freeze your line if home values decline or your financial situation changes.
  • Closing Costs: HELOCs typically carry closing costs including appraisal, title, and recording fees.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a HELOC and how does it differ from a home equity loan?
A HELOC (Home Equity Line of Credit) is a revolving line of credit secured by your home. Unlike a home equity loan — which gives you a lump sum at a fixed rate — a HELOC lets you draw funds up to your credit limit during a set draw period (usually 10 years), then repay over a repayment period (usually 20 years). You only pay interest on what you actually borrow, and as you repay, your available credit is restored.
How much can I borrow with a HELOC?
Your borrowing limit depends on your home's appraised value, your existing mortgage balance, your credit score, and your income. AmeriSave can finance up to 90% of your home's combined loan-to-value (CLTV). For example, if your home is worth $400,000 and you owe $250,000, you may be able to access up to $110,000 (90% × $400,000 = $360,000 − $250,000 = $110,000).
What are the current HELOC interest rates?
HELOC rates are variable and typically tied to the Prime Rate plus a margin determined by your creditworthiness, equity, and lender. Rates fluctuate with Federal Reserve policy. The best way to see your personalized rate is to apply — AmeriSave will provide you a customized quote based on your specific financial profile and home equity.
How long does it take to get a HELOC?
The timeline varies but typically ranges from 2 to 6 weeks from application to closing. This includes the time to complete the application, appraise your home, underwrite the loan, and schedule closing. AmeriSave's streamlined digital process helps reduce delays — having all your documentation ready (income verification, tax returns, mortgage statement) can speed things up significantly.
Can I lock in a fixed rate on a HELOC?
Some lenders offer a "rate lock" or "fixed-rate advance" feature that lets you convert all or a portion of your HELOC balance to a fixed rate. This can provide payment predictability for specific draws. Ask an AmeriSave loan officer about the rate lock options available on your HELOC.
What happens when the draw period ends?
Once the draw period ends (typically after 10 years), the HELOC enters the repayment period — usually 20 years. You can no longer draw funds, and your monthly payments increase to cover both principal and interest on the outstanding balance. It's important to plan for this payment increase ahead of time. Some borrowers choose to refinance at this stage.
Is HELOC interest tax-deductible?
Under current IRS rules (as of 2018 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act), interest on a HELOC is deductible only if the loan proceeds are used to "buy, build, or substantially improve" the home securing the loan. Interest used for other purposes (debt consolidation, personal expenses) is generally not deductible. Always consult a qualified tax advisor to understand the deductibility for your situation.
