Simple Process

How It Works

Getting a HELOC with AmeriSave is straightforward. Here's what to expect.

1

Apply Online

Complete our quick, secure online application. You'll provide basic information about yourself, your home, and your financial situation. Most applications take under 15 minutes.

2

Get Approved

Our team reviews your application, verifies your home's equity position, and determines your credit line. We'll walk you through your rate and terms before you commit to anything.

3

Close & Access Your Line

Once approved and closed, your credit line is available. Draw funds whenever you need them via check, transfer, or a dedicated card — up to your approved limit during your draw period.

4

Draw, Repay & Repeat

During your 10-year draw period, borrow what you need and repay it. As you pay down the balance, funds become available again — giving you ongoing financial flexibility.

5

Repayment Period Begins

After your draw period ends, the repayment period begins (typically 20 years). You'll make principal + interest payments until the balance is paid in full.

Do You Qualify?

Eligibility Requirements

Most homeowners with equity qualify. Here are the key criteria AmeriSave evaluates.

Credit Score

A minimum credit score of 680 is generally required. Higher scores may qualify for better rates and terms.

Sufficient Home Equity

You typically need at least 15–20% equity in your home. AmeriSave can lend up to 90% combined loan-to-value (CLTV).

Debt-to-Income Ratio (DTI)

Your combined monthly debt obligations should generally not exceed 43% of your gross monthly income.

Stable Income & Employment

Lenders look for consistent income and employment history to confirm your ability to repay. Self-employed applicants are welcome.

Primary or Secondary Residence

HELOCs are available on primary residences and, in many cases, second homes. Investment properties may have different requirements.

Required Documentation

Be prepared with recent pay stubs, W-2s or tax returns, mortgage statements, proof of homeowners insurance, and a government-issued ID.

Balanced View

HELOC Pros & Cons

We believe in full transparency. Here's an honest look at HELOCs.

Pros

  • Lower Interest Rates

    Typically much lower than credit cards and unsecured personal loans.

  • Only Pay for What You Use

    Interest accrues only on the amount drawn, not your full credit line.

  • Large Credit Lines Available

    Access up to 90% of your home's LTV for significant financial needs.

  • Potential Tax Deductions

    Interest may be deductible if used for home improvements (consult a tax advisor).

  • Flexible Repayment Options

    Interest-only payments during draw period help manage monthly cash flow.

Cons

  • Variable Interest Rate Risk

    Rates can rise with the market, increasing your monthly payment unexpectedly.

  • Home Is Collateral

    Defaulting on a HELOC puts your home at risk of foreclosure.

  • Payment Shock at Repayment

    When the draw period ends, payments increase significantly as principal is added.

  • Lender Can Reduce or Freeze Line

    In certain economic conditions, lenders may freeze or reduce access to funds.

  • Closing Costs

    There are upfront costs (appraisal, origination fees) to establish a HELOC.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

A HELOC is a revolving line of credit — like a credit card secured by your home — where you borrow as needed and only pay interest on what you draw. A home equity loan is a lump-sum loan with fixed payments. A HELOC offers flexibility; a home equity loan offers payment predictability.

AmeriSave allows you to borrow up to 90% of your home's combined loan-to-value (CLTV). For example, if your home is worth $400,000 and your mortgage balance is $200,000, you may be eligible for a credit line up to $160,000. Exact amounts depend on your credit profile, income, and property value.

HELOC rates are typically variable, tied to the prime rate (or another benchmark index) plus a margin. This means your rate — and monthly payment — can change over time. Some lenders offer the ability to convert a portion of your balance to a fixed rate. Ask your loan officer for details.

The HELOC process typically takes 2–6 weeks from application to funding. This includes application review, home appraisal, underwriting, and closing. AmeriSave's digital-first process is designed to move as quickly as possible.

Generally, yes. Common uses include home improvements, debt consolidation, education costs, medical expenses, and emergency funds. There is no restriction on how you spend the funds, though using them for home improvements may have tax benefits.

After the draw period (typically 10 years), you enter the repayment period (typically 20 years). You can no longer draw funds, and monthly payments increase to cover both principal and interest on the outstanding balance. Planning ahead for this transition is important.

Getting a rate estimate typically involves a soft credit pull that does not affect your score. A formal application will require a hard credit inquiry, which may cause a small, temporary dip in your credit score. This is standard practice across all mortgage products.

HELOCs typically come with closing costs including appraisal fees, origination fees, title fees, and recording fees. Some lenders charge annual fees or inactivity fees. AmeriSave will clearly disclose all fees before you commit, so there are no surprises.
