Why a HELOC?

Key Benefits of a HELOC

Borrow smarter. A HELOC gives you a revolving line of credit at lower interest rates than personal loans or credit cards — secured by the equity you've already built.

Flexible Credit Line

Access funds up to your credit limit anytime during your draw period — borrow, repay, and borrow again as your needs change.

Competitive Interest Rates

HELOCs typically carry lower rates than credit cards or personal loans because they're secured by your home's equity.

Interest-Only Payments

During your draw period, pay interest only on what you've borrowed — keeping monthly payments lower while you access funds.

10-Year Draw Period

Use your credit line for up to 10 years. Take funds in stages rather than all at once — ideal for ongoing projects or expenses.

Leverage Your Home's Value

Your home's equity is a powerful financial asset. A HELOC lets you put that built-up value to work for you — at favorable terms.

Potential Tax Benefits

Interest paid on a HELOC may be tax-deductible when funds are used for home improvements. Consult your tax advisor for details.

Do You Qualify?

Eligibility Requirements

Most homeowners with sufficient equity qualify for a HELOC. Here's what lenders typically evaluate:

  • Sufficient Home Equity

    You typically need at least 15–20% equity in your home. Lenders usually allow you to borrow up to 85% of your home's appraised value, minus what you owe.

  • Credit Score of 620 or Higher

    A minimum credit score of 620 is generally required, though a higher score (700+) can help you qualify for better interest rates.

  • Debt-to-Income Ratio Below 43%

    Your total monthly debt payments (including the new HELOC) should not exceed 43% of your gross monthly income.

  • Primary or Secondary Residence

    HELOCs are available on primary and secondary homes. Investment properties may have different requirements.

  • Reliable Income History

    Lenders want to see steady income — typically 2 years of employment history or self-employment documentation — to verify repayment ability.

Balanced View

HELOC Pros & Cons

We believe in transparency. Here's an honest look at the advantages and considerations of a HELOC so you can make an informed decision.

Advantages

  • Flexible borrowing

    Draw funds as needed rather than all at once — pay interest only on what you actually use.

  • Lower initial rates

    Variable rates are typically lower than fixed home equity loans and significantly lower than credit cards.

  • Interest-only payment option

    During the 10-year draw period, you may have the option to make interest-only payments, reducing cash flow pressure.

  • Potential tax deductibility

    Interest may be tax-deductible if funds are used to improve your home. Consult your tax advisor.

  • Reusable credit line

    As you repay, the credit becomes available again — similar to a credit card, but backed by your home's equity.

Considerations

  • Variable interest rates

    Rates can rise over time as market conditions change, which may increase your monthly payments.

  • Home is used as collateral

    Failure to repay puts your home at risk of foreclosure. Borrow responsibly within your means.

  • Payment increase after draw period

    Once the draw period ends, you enter repayment and must pay both principal and interest, which increases monthly payments.

  • Lender may freeze or reduce line

    In some market conditions, lenders can reduce your available credit line — potentially limiting your access to funds.

  • Closing costs and fees

    Like any mortgage product, a HELOC involves closing costs, appraisal fees, and potentially annual fees.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about HELOCs — answered clearly.

What is a HELOC and how does it work?
A Home Equity Line of Credit (HELOC) is a revolving credit line secured by your home's equity. Similar to a credit card, you can draw funds up to your credit limit, repay them, and borrow again during a set draw period — typically 10 years. After the draw period, you enter a repayment phase (usually 20 years) during which you pay down both principal and interest.
How much can I borrow with a HELOC?
The amount you can borrow depends on your home's appraised value, how much you still owe on your mortgage, and your creditworthiness. Most lenders, including AmeriSave, allow you to borrow up to 85% of your home's value minus your outstanding mortgage balance. For example, if your home is worth $400,000 and you owe $250,000, you may be eligible for a credit line of up to $90,000.
What is the difference between the draw period and repayment period?
During the draw period (typically up to 10 years), you can access funds from your credit line and are generally only required to make interest payments on the amount borrowed. After the draw period ends, you enter the repayment period (typically up to 20 years), during which you can no longer draw new funds and must make full principal-and-interest payments to pay off the outstanding balance.
Are HELOC interest rates fixed or variable?
Most HELOCs carry variable interest rates, meaning the rate can change over time based on an index (such as the Prime Rate). This means your payments can fluctuate. Some lenders offer the ability to convert a portion of your balance to a fixed rate. If payment predictability is important to you, a Home Equity Loan with a fixed rate may be a better option.
Will a HELOC application affect my credit score?
Getting a rate estimate or exploring your options with AmeriSave does not impact your credit score — we use a soft credit inquiry for initial qualification. A formal application will result in a hard inquiry, which can have a small, temporary impact on your score. Shopping multiple lenders within a short window (typically 14–45 days) is treated as a single inquiry by most credit scoring models.
Can I pay off my HELOC early?
Yes. You can make additional payments at any time during the draw or repayment period without penalty (check your loan terms for any prepayment fees). Paying down the principal during the draw period reduces the amount you owe and increases available credit. Paying off early can save you significant interest over the life of the line.
Is the interest on a HELOC tax deductible?
Under current tax law, interest paid on a HELOC may be tax-deductible if the funds are used to "buy, build, or substantially improve" the home securing the loan. Interest used for other purposes (e.g., debt consolidation, personal expenses) is generally not deductible. Tax laws change, so we strongly recommend consulting a qualified tax advisor for advice specific to your situation.
How long does it take to get a HELOC with AmeriSave?
The HELOC process typically takes 2–6 weeks from application to closing. The timeline depends on factors like the speed of your home appraisal, document gathering, and underwriting review. AmeriSave's streamlined online process and dedicated loan officers are designed to move as efficiently as possible while ensuring accuracy and compliance.
